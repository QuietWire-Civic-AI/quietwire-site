The promise

Small enough to finish. Real enough to matter.

The pilot does not attempt to automate the organization. It chooses one consequential working loop and proves that local custody, useful AI assistance, governed memory, and inspectable evidence can live together.

The path

Five phases. One continuous record.

Days 1–7

Frame

Walk the real workflow. Define purpose, users, authority, data boundaries, failure conditions, and success criteria.

  • working-system map
  • trust-boundary statement
  • pilot acceptance criteria
Days 8–21

Prepare

Source or confirm hardware, burn in the node, harden access, establish backup, and prepare approved content.

  • appliance build record
  • configuration baseline
  • content intake register
Days 22–32

Install

Place the node, connect the approved workflow, establish identities and roles, and demonstrate the first bounded loop.

  • installed node
  • first workflow
  • operator orientation
Days 33–52

Prove

Use it with real work. Tune retrieval and interfaces. Test refusal, failure, recovery, export, and human review.

  • use and issue log
  • evidence receipts
  • recovery demonstration
Days 53–60

Decide

Review outcomes, hand over the operating record, and decide whether to steward, expand, redesign, or stop cleanly.

  • verification report
  • 30/60/90 roadmap
  • expansion decision

A good first workflow

Consequential, bounded, and already understood by the people doing it.

  • Repeats often enough to learn from
  • Depends on documents, decisions, or handoffs
  • Has a human owner who can review output
  • Benefits from continuity across staff or time
  • Can be demonstrated without exposing prohibited data
  • Has an honest stopping condition

Not the first pilot

Do not begin with everything.

We will not propose autonomous authority, enterprise-wide ingestion, unrestricted memory, or a silent replacement for a regulated system of record as the first move.

One useful loop is enough to establish the truth.
